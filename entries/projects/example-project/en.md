This project investigates heat and fluid transport inside a packed-bed methanol steam reforming reactor using computational fluid dynamics (CFD). The analysis compares a baseline reactor with a configuration containing passive flow disturbers.

## Problem

Methanol steam reforming is an endothermic process, and heat must be transferred from the heated reactor wall into the catalyst bed. Large radial temperature gradients can reduce reactor performance, making heat transfer inside the packed bed an important design consideration.

## Approach

A CFD model was used to analyze temperature and velocity distributions inside the reactor.

Two configurations were compared:

- Baseline reactor without flow disturbers
- Reactor with passive ring-and-disk flow disturbers

The analysis focused on how the flow-disturber geometry changes internal flow patterns and heat transport within the catalyst bed.

## Result

The CFD results showed clear differences in both temperature and velocity distributions between the baseline and flow-disturber configurations.

The flow disturbers redirected the flow within the packed bed and altered the local velocity field, providing a basis for evaluating their effect on radial heat transfer and reactor performance.
