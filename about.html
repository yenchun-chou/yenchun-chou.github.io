PhD Research

Coupled Heat and Mass Transfer, Reaction Modeling, and Design Optimization of Packed-Bed Reactors

My doctoral research investigates how fluid flow, heat and mass transfer, and chemical reactions interact to determine the performance of packed-bed methanol steam reformers. The central challenge is improving heat delivery and reactant transport within the catalyst bed while managing pressure drop, methanol conversion, and hydrogen production.

Multiphysics CFD and reaction kinetics. I developed a multi-region fluid-solid CFD model in OpenFOAM, incorporating momentum, energy, and species transport with Peppley Langmuir-Hinshelwood kinetics for methanol steam reforming, water-gas shift, and methanol decomposition. I adapted effective kinetic parameters to the KMA catalyst and evaluated model predictions against experimental measurements across multiple flow rates.

Model verification and experimental validation. My modeling work includes non-reacting heat-transfer verification, reaction-kinetics evaluation, mesh convergence assessment, and CFD-experiment comparisons. I also programmed LabVIEW data acquisition with gas-analysis instrumentation and used Python and MATLAB to analyze reactor measurements and simulation results.

Parametric design and optimization. I conducted a 52-case CFD investigation across four flow rates, incorporating an L9 Taguchi design, additional comparison configurations, and a baseline without flow disturbers. The study examined how disk and ring dimensions, insert count, and spacing influence flow redistribution, radial temperature gradients, pressure drop, and overall reactor performance.

Experimental performance and industry collaboration. In related research with H₂ PowerTech, our team evaluated methanol reformers with different aspect ratios and operating conditions, investigating the relationships among conversion, carbon monoxide formation, and hydrogen production. This work contributed to reactor design and scale-up considerations and resulted in my first-author publication in the International Journal of Hydrogen Energy (2025).
