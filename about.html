Background

I work on thermal-fluid systems where simulation and hardware have to agree.

PhD Research

Heat Transfer Enhancement and Flow Management in Packed-Bed Methanol Steam Reformers

Methanol steam reforming is an endothermic process in which heat must be transferred from the reactor wall into a catalyst-packed bed. Nonuniform flow and limited radial heat transfer can create cooler regions near the centerline, reducing reactor performance.

My research examines whether passive disk-and-ring flow disturbers can redistribute flow, improve radial heat transfer, and reduce temperature nonuniformity without moving components. I use CFD and reactor-scale thermal modeling to investigate flow behavior, temperature distributions, and reaction performance.

A central part of this work is evaluating numerical predictions against experimental measurements and examining how design parameters, including disk diameter, ring geometry, spacing, and insert count, affect reactor behavior.

In related research with H₂ PowerTech, the team investigated the relationship between methanol conversion, carbon monoxide (CO) formation, and hydrogen production in reactors with different geometries and operating conditions. This collaboration led to my first-author publication in the International Journal of Hydrogen Energy (2025).

I also co-authored a review article in Fuel (2026), examining approaches to enhance hydrogen production from methanol steam reforming, with an emphasis on heat-transfer limitations and reactor-scale enhancement strategies.

How I approach engineering problems

The design that performs best on one metric is not always the best overall solution.

In my methanol reformer research, increasing wall temperature improved conversion but also increased CO formation. Passive flow disturbers reduced CO concentration from 4.5% to 1.6% while maintaining comparable conversion and increasing hydrogen production.
