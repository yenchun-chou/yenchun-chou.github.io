MECHANICAL ENGINEERING · THERMAL-FLUID SYSTEMS

About me

Mechanical Engineer (EIT) and PhD Candidate in Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering at the University of California, Davis. Expected graduation: December 2026.

Background

I work on thermal-fluid systems where simulation and hardware have to agree.

In collaboration with H₂ PowerTech, I studied how passive disk-and-ring flow disturbers affect heat transfer, carbon monoxide (CO) formation, and hydrogen production in packed-bed methanol steam reformers. This work led to a first-author publication in the International Journal of Hydrogen Energy (2025).

My experience also includes operating high-temperature fixed-bed reactors at temperatures up to 800 °C as a visiting researcher at UC San Diego, and working on a 500 lb/hr biomass-to-renewable-natural-gas pilot plant at West Biofuels.

I hold an MS and BS in Mechanical Engineering from National Taiwan University of Science and Technology and passed the FE Mechanical examination in 2026.

What I work on

CFD and heat transfer: I develop and evaluate thermal-fluid models using OpenFOAM and ANSYS Fluent, including conjugate heat transfer, reaction kinetics, turbulence modeling, and parametric geometry studies. My validation approach includes mesh convergence analysis and comparison with experimental measurements.

Experimental systems: My hands-on experience includes operating high-temperature reactor test stands, acquiring thermocouple and gas-composition measurements, LabVIEW/DAQ automation, mass flow controller calibration, and troubleshooting gas-handling equipment.

Mechanical design: I use SolidWorks and other CAD tools to translate thermal and fluid-flow requirements into practical hardware, including reactor assemblies and passive flow-disturber components.

Technical skills

Simulation: OpenFOAM, ANSYS Fluent, conjugate heat transfer, turbulence modeling, mesh convergence, Grid Convergence Index (GCI)

Programming & Data Acquisition: Python, MATLAB, LabVIEW, DAQ

Mechanical Design & CAD: SolidWorks, AutoCAD, Creo

Languages: English, Mandarin Chinese

How I approach engineering problems

The design that performs best on one metric is not always the best overall solution.

In my methanol reformer research, increasing wall temperature improved conversion but also increased CO formation. Passive flow disturbers reduced CO concentration from 4.5% to 1.6% while maintaining comparable conversion and increasing hydrogen production.

Selected work

Methanol Reformer Development with Passive Flow Disturbers — Experimental research on thermal behavior, CO formation, and hydrogen production in packed-bed reformers. Published in the International Journal of Hydrogen Energy.

CFD Analysis of Heat and Flow in a Packed-Bed Reactor — Numerical investigation of passive flow disturbance, radial heat transfer, and temperature distribution in packed-bed reformers.

Liquid Cooling of High-Power Electronics — OpenFOAM-13 conjugate heat transfer validation of a 400 W cold plate, including a three-level mesh convergence study.

Experimental Toluene Steam Reforming for Biomass Tar Removal — High-temperature fixed-bed reactor testing and catalyst performance evaluation for biomass tar removal.

Professional interests

I am pursuing engineering opportunities in thermal-fluid systems, mechanical design, product development, and test and validation, particularly roles that combine simulation with hands-on hardware development.
